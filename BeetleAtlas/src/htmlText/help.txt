	<!-- 1.1 What is BeetleAtlas for? -->
	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt1_1','hs1_1','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt1_1" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> What is BeetleAtlas for?
</div><!-- end of 1st subtitle div -->
<div id="hs1_1" class="conceal helpContent">
The primary purpose of BeetleAtlas is to show which genes are transcribed in individual tissues and at different life stages of <em>Tribolium castaneum</em>, the red flour beetle. The experimental basis for this is RNA-Seq data.
</div><!-- end of 1st content div -->

	<!-- 1.2 Why Two Modes? -->
	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt1_2','hs1_2','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt1_2" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> What are the Two Modes?
</div><!-- end of 2nd subtitle div -->
<div id="hs1_2" class="conceal helpContent">
Raw RNAseq reads are assigned to regions of the assembled genome. Interpretation of this as transcription of specific genes depends on the annotation of the genome on the basis of experimental evidence and computer prediction. There are two main sets of annotations or gene models — the RefSeq annotation on the NCBI/NLM website, and the Official Gene Set (OGS3) annotation hosted on the iBeetle-Base website. The NCBI annotations use numeric gene IDs, whereas the OGS3 annotations use alpha-numeric gene IDs of the type ‘TCxxxxxx’. For BeetleAtlas 2 the same raw RNAseq reads were processed separately against the  reference genomes with the two different annotations and the results presented as two modes — the OGS3 mode (as in the original BeetleAtlas) and the NCBI mode (new in BeetleAtlas 2). Although both original annotations used the Tcas 5.2 genome assembly, the current NCBI annotation is for the more recent icTricast1.1 assembly. 
</div><!-- end of 2nd content div -->

	<!-- 1.3 How different are the results from the two modes? -->
	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt1_3','hs1_3','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt1_3" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Can one Convert between the two types of ID?
</div><!-- end of 3rd subtitle div -->
<div id="hs1_3" class="conceal helpContent">
BeetleAtlas includes protein-coding genes from the cytoplasmic genome. There are 16593 of these in the OGS3 model and 13258 in the NCBI model, so it is obvious that in many cases a simple 1:1 equivalence cannot exist. Indeed the icTricast1.1 NCBI annotation only includes OSG3 equivalences for 598 genes. We have drawn up a somewhat larger list of 8391 on the criterion of unique correlation in all three cases when BLAST was run on predicted proteins, mRNAs and coding sequences. This is a conservative estimate, although it does not preclude small exon differences. 
</div><!-- end of 3rd content div -->

	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt1_4','hs1_4','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt1_4" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Correlating Results from the Two Modes
</div><!-- end of 4th subtitle div -->
<div id="hs1_4" class="conceal helpContent">
For those ca. 8000 genes with proposed equivalences, the corresponding gene in the alternative annotation is included in the headers of query results tables. Clicking on the ID will open a window with the results for the alternative mode so that the user can check whether there really is a single consistent tissue-specific pattern of gene expression. Where no equivalent is given in the results, a link to a custom view of the RNAseq reads in the UCSC Genome Browser can be used. This allows users to make their own assessment of whether an equivalent exists and to find the reasons for apparent differences in tissue-specific expression of genes programmatically designated as equivalent.
</div><!-- end of 4th content div -->

	<!-- 1.5 So is BeetleAtlas also a tool for testing the Gene Models? -->
	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt1_5','hs1_5','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt1_5" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> BeetleAtlas 2 can be used to refine Gene Models
</div><!-- end of 5th subtitle div -->
<div id="hs1_5" class="conceal helpContent">
Although primarily designed to study tissue-specific gene transcription, the customized UCSC Genome Browser presentation in BeetleAtlas 2 allows one to flip the coin, as it were, and confirm or reconcile the different gene models. This approach assumes that the exons of a single transcript will all display the same pattern of tissue expression, and that adjacent genes will generally exhibit different patterns of tissue-specific expresson. Despite its limitations, we believe this is a powerful approach for studying this model organism, the genetics of which are less developed than <em>Drosophila melanogaster</em>. More details can be found under ‘The UCSC Browser View’.
</div><!-- end of 5th content div -->

</div><!-- end of conceal div -->

<div class="helpTitle">
<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2','hs2','&#9658;','&#9660;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2" class="helpTitle onOff">▶</span></a> General Instructions for Use
</div>
<div id="hs2" class="conceal">

	<!-- 2.1 Main Search Types -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_1','hs2_1','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_1" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Main Search Types
	</div>
	<div id="hs2_1" class="conceal helpContent">
	Six main types of search can be performed after selecting the appropriate section from the menu. <strong>Gene</strong> searches are made for a particular gene using identifiers, symbols or descriptions of the gene product. Searches can also be made for genes analogous to those in <em>Drosophila melanogaster</em> using standard fly identifiers; <strong>Tissue</strong> searches produce a list of the most abundant or enriched genes in a particular tissue; <strong>Category</strong> searches are made using keywords assigned to certain classes of genes by the authors; <strong>Adult/Larval</strong>  searches allow one to find genes which show marked differences in expression between adult and larva; <strong>Embryo</strong> searches allow one to look for genes that are expressed at a particular time in embryo development, and those not expressed in adults and larvae; and <strong>Profile</strong> searches allow one to find genes with a similar pattern of tissue expression to a gene of interest.
	</div>
	
		<!-- 2.2 Autosuggest -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_2','hs2_2','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_2" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why You should use Autosuggest</div>
	<div id="hs2_2" class="conceal helpContent">For every field in which a gene name or identifier is entered there is an autocomplete facility based on the entries in the database underlying BeetleAtlas. The autosuggest for a Symbol starts after two characters and that for a gene ID after six. The reason why you should use this is to save yourself the frustration of making a query, only to receive a message that the gene is not in the database. If the gene is not in the autocomplete menu you can be sure that it is <em>not</em> in the database.</div>
		<!-- 2.4 Abundance and Enrichment -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_4','hs2_4','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_4" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Abundance and Enrichment</div>
	<div id="hs2_4" class="conceal helpContent">In searches in which a list of genes is retrieved one is generally offered the choice of ranking these by abundance or enrichment. <strong>Abundance</strong> is simply the FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads). The <strong>enrichment</strong> is a measure of the abundance of a gene in a particular tissue relative to that in the whole beetle. This allows one to determine whether the expression of a particular gene is tissue-specific or not. In order to avoid division by zero and misleading values of enrichment, in calculating the latter it is necessary to set abundance values for whole beetles that fall below the background threshold to that of the threshold (2 FPKM). This may result in quantitative underestimations, so users should inspect the data carefully. Enrichments are not presented for individual transcripts. </div>
	<!-- 2.12 Category Search -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_12','hs2_12','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_12" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> The Categories in Category Searches </div>
	<div id="hs2_12" class="conceal helpContent">The categories offered in Category Searches are not base on any official classification, as at the time of building this application no Tribolium Gene Ontology is available. The categories encompass a small number of keywords that describe features of interest to us that we have manually assigned to genes on the basis of their names or our knowledge of their functions. They will contain mistakes and omissions. We have deliberately excluded housekeeping genes such as those for ribosomal proteins because these are expressed in all the tissues here. If you have suggestions for the addition of other categories of interest to you, please contact us using the feedback form.
</div>	
		<!-- 2.5 Heat Maps -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_5','hs2_5','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_5" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> What the Colours Mean</div>
	<div id="hs2_5" class="conceal helpContent">The colouring of cell backgrounds (heat mapping) is merely a visual aid to help users quickly identify the results of most interest. The colouring is not a surrogate for the numerical data, and in the case of transcripts, the user should download the tables to inspect values of interest. The scales have been devised to accomodate the particular ranges of values in the database. For abundance values the white-to-black spectrum illustrated below is used. For FPKMs this has a 15-step log1.6 scale, but all values below 2 FPKM (considered background) are coloured white, and no discrimination is employed for values above 1150 FPKM (pure black).<br />
	<img src="images/FPKMabundanceSpectrum.png" alt="" width="460" height="54" /><br />
	For enrichment values a divergent white/yellow/red spectrum is used:<br />
	<img src="images/enrichmentSpectrum.png" alt="" width="450" height="54" /><br />
	This employs an asymmetric log scale running from white to yellow (0-1) to represent genes with ‘decreased enrichment’, and then from yellow through orange and a wider range of reds to encompass values of ‘increased enrichment’.
	</div>
		<!-- 2.6 Results Options -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_6','hs2_6','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_6" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Result Presentation Options</div>
	<div id="hs2_6" class="conceal helpContent">The default presentation of the table containing the results of a query has been simplified for initial ease of inspection. Checkboxes provide options of more detailed information. <strong>SD</strong> appends standard deviations (based on triplicates) to the values. <strong>Whole Body</strong> includes the abundance values for whole insects. Checkbox selections are retained in subsequent queries until changed.</div>
		<!-- 2.10 correlating gene and transcript data -->
		<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_10','hs2_10','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_10" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Correlating Gene and Transcript data</div>
	<div id="hs2_10" class="conceal helpContent">The table with the transcript data is, of necessity, quite compact. However it is possible to highlight the part of the <em>transcript</em> table corresponding to a particular tissue by clicking/tapping on the cell in question in the <em>gene</em> table. Click/tap again to turn the highlighting off.</div>  
		<!-- 2.7 Downloading Tables -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_7','hs2_7','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_7" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Downloading Tables</div>
	<div id="hs2_7" class="conceal helpContent">There are download icons below each of the tables of gene and transcript data. These generate files in <em>tab-separated</em> format with an extension ‘txt’. Such files will likely open in a text editor when double-clicked, but can be imported into open spreadsheet applications, such as <em>MS Excel</em>, <em>LibreOffice</em> or Apple's <em>Numbers</em>. The files can then be formatted and saved in the application’s native format, if required.</div>
		<!-- 2.3 Downloading Graphs -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_3','hs2_3','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_3" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Downloading Graphs</div>
	<div id="hs2_3" class="conceal helpContent">There is a button to download the bar-charts in SVG format. This is a vector graphics format which, unlike the bitmap formats, jpg or png, will scale without distortion. However a vector graphics application, such as Adobe <em>Illustrator</em>, is needed to handle svg files. If you want, or can only handle, bitmap graphics, take a screenshot.</div>	
	
		<!-- 2.8 iBeetle-Base and NCBI -->
	<div class="helpSubT">
	<a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt2_8','hs2_8','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt2_8" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Links to External Documentation</div>	
	<div id="hs2_8" class="conceal helpContent">
		In OSG3 mode, clicking on the TC ID loads the relevant page of <em>iBeetle-Base</em> in a new window. In NCBI mode, clicking on the NCBI ID loads the relevant page at NCBI/NLM in a new window. Among other things, these provide sequence information and visualization of the gene in different genome browsers. (Note that clicking on an NCBI ID in OSG3 mode, or on an OSG3 ID in NCBI mode does not link out but opens a window with the results ofgenomic assemblies the other mode.)
</div>

<div id="hs6" class="conceal">
	<!-- 3.1 About the UCSC Browser -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt6_1','hs6_1','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt6_1" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why the UCSC Browser?</div>
	<div id="hs6_1" class="conceal helpContent">
	The UCSC Genome Browser (https://genome.ucsc.edu) is a facility to ”Interactively visualize genomic data“ for a wide variety of species and for different genome assemblies. Parallel to the chromosome/sequence graphic several annotation ’tracks’ are provided with information such as gene models, repeat regions and CpG Islands. The reason we have used the UCSC Browser with BeetleAtlas 2 is that it allows us to provide a custom view of a particular region of the genome <em>with our own added tracks</em>. UCSC hosts the Genome Browser, but pulls the custom tracks from our BeetleAtlas server.
	</div>
	
	<!-- 3.5 Custom RNAseq tracks -->	
		<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt6_5','hs6_5','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt6_5" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Custom RNAseq tracks</div>
	<div id="hs6_5" class="conceal helpContent">
	The first type of custom track shows the coverage of RNAseq reads for each tissue or life stage. This is qualitative, rather than quantitative — BeetleAtlas provides the quantitation. However it allows the user to determine visually whether the gene model used for the quantitation is consistent with the position and tissue-specificity of the RNAseq reads.<br />
	<img width="600" height="276" src="images/rnaseqTracks.png" alt="" />
		</div>
	
	<!-- 3.6 Custom Gene Model Tracks -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt6_6','hs6_6','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt6_6" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Custom Gene Model Tracks</div>
	<div id="hs6_6" class="conceal helpContent">
	Of the available genome assemblies which the UCSC Genome Browser offers for <em>Tribolium castaneum</em> we have chosen the most recent: icTriCast1.1. A track for the NCBI gene model is provided with this. The OSG3 gene model with which we wished to allow comparison was determined using the earlier Tcas5.2 genome assembly, but we were able to use the ‘Liftoff’ program to map the major part of it to icTriCast1.1. From this we created a custom track for our UCSC Browser view. We have done likewise with approx. 1000 NCBI gene predictions from the Tcas5.2 assembly that have been discontinued in icTriCast1.1. Finally, we have substituted a single custom track for the current NCBI gene model provided by UCSC, and labelled it with gene symbols rather than transcript IDs. (Transcript IDs can be seen by hovering over the gene, or the default tracks can be restored if the user wishes.)<br />
	<img width="400" height="91" src="images/genemodelTracks.png" alt="" />
	</div>
	
	<!-- 3.7 Reconciling Differences in Gene Models -->

	<div id="hs6_7" class="conceal helpContent">In general one may be able to resolve differences between gene models by correlating them to the raw RNAseq reads in different tissues. In the example below the reads can be seen to favour the OSG3 gene model of two genes — TC10814, which is expressed in many tissues, and TC10815 (similar to the discontinued NCBI gene 103313646), which is expressed in adult head and rectum.<br />

		<!-- 3.8 Tips on using the Custom UCSC Browser Interface -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt6_8','hs6_8','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt6_8" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Tips on using the UCSC Browser Interface</div>
	<div id="hs6_8" class="conceal helpContent">
<ul>
	<li>ZOOM: The UCSC Browser usually opens with the gene of interest occupying most of the width of the window. You generally need to zoom out 3x or 10x to inspect a gene in the context of its neighbours: <br />
	<img width="439" height="39" src="images/zoom.png" alt="" />
	</li>
	<li>SCALES: All tracks in the UCSC Browser are the same physical height, so it is necessary to refer to the scale at the left for a quantitative impression of RNAseq reads. This is especially important when there is much higher expression in one particular tissue for an adjacent gene, which in such cases needs to be scrolled or zoomed out of view.<br />
	<img width="193" height="160" src="images/scale.jpg" alt="" />
	</li>
	<li>REORDERING LANES: Often one is interested to compare just in a few of the many conditions presented. One may bring these together by dragging their left bars up or down or clicking on the bar to hide those that are not of immediate interest.<br />
	<img width="386" height="160" src="images/reorder.jpg" alt="" /></li>
	<li>RESTORE DEFAULT TRACKS: The default tracks have been hidden to simplify the interface but you may wish to reverse this. For example showing the repeat regions may be relevant to raw RNAseq reads for which there are no corresponding predicted genes. There are options to do this at the bottom of the window.<br />
	<img width="258" height="143" src="images/defaults.png" alt="" />
	</li>
	<li>RESET: If you reorganize the tracks for one particular gene, the UCSC Browser makes this the default organization for subsequent genes. To reset so that the view will be that provided by BeetleAtlas, choose the ‘Reset all user settings’ option from the ‘Genome Browser’ menu. (The effect of this is somewhat disconcerting as it takes you to the UCSC home page. Close this and return to the link in BeetleAtlas.)<br />
	<img width="205" height="147" src="images/reset.png" alt="" />
	</li>
</ul>

	<!-- 3.1 Gene not in database -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt3_1','hs3_1','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt3_1" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why is my Gene not in the Database?</div>
	<div id="hs3_1" class="conceal helpContent">First, make sure that the appropriate name or identifier has been selected, start typing, and check the autocomplete for related spellings. If possible, use a gene ID rather than a gene symbol as the latter may change but the former should not. Note that NCBI IDs are not preceded by ’LOC’, which is sometimes used in gene symbols. It should also be emphasized that BeetleAtlas only contains transcripts of protein-coding genes and excludes those encoded by the mitochondrial genome.</div>
	<!-- 3.5 NA -->	
		<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt3_5','hs3_5','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt3_5" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why are some results marked ‘N.A.’?</div>
	<div id="hs3_5" class="conceal helpContent">Generally this is because the data are ‘Not Available’ as the program used to process the raw data flagged their status as other than ‘OK’. Often this is because the values are very high. In other cases ‘N.A.’ is used where no value of enhancement has been presented because the FPKM value was less than 2, the value used as for cutoff above background.</div>
	<!-- 3.2 Fly equivalents differ -->	
		<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt3_2','hs3_2','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt3_2" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why do <em>Drosophila</em> Homologues differ in the Two Modes?</div>
	<div id="hs3_2" class="conceal helpContent">The <em>Drosophila</em> homologues for the OGS3 mode were kindly provided by Gregor Bucher of iBeetle-Base. We constructed a separate set of homologies for the NCBI mode, for which OrthoFinder and eggNOG were used. Ultimately the user must decide whether specific genes from different species are indeed homologous.</div>
	<!-- 3.6 Mobile limitations -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt3_6','hs3_6','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt3_6" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Why are some Features not available on Mobile?</div>
	<div id="hs3_6" class="conceal helpContent">We designed the BeetleAtlas web application so that it responded to the decrease in viewing area of mobile devices by maintaining core  functionality but dispensing with some secondary features. Selecting ‘Request Desktop Site’ from the options in your mobile web browser may — depending on your device — give you access to these features.</div>

<div id="hs4" class="conceal">
	<!-- 4.1 Insects and Tissues -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt4_1','hs4_1','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt4_1" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Insects &amp; Tissues</div>
	<div id="hs4_1" class="conceal helpContent"><em>Tribolium castaneum</em> (San Bernardino strain) stocks were maintained on organic whole-wheat flour supplemented with 5% (w/w) yeast powder at 30 °C at a constant 50% relative humidity and 12:12 light–dark cycles. The tissues are illustrated in the ‘Anatomy’ graphic, available on each results page.</div>
	<!-- 4.2 RNA-Seq -->		
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt4_2','hs4_2','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt4_2" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> RNA-Seq</div>
	<div id="hs4_2" class="conceal helpContent">RNA was isolated in triplicate using the Qiagen miRNeasy kit. The quality of RNA samples was analysed using an Agilent Bioanalyzer.</div>
	<!-- 4.3 Computational Analysis -->
	<div class="helpSubT"><a href="javascript:toggleConcealed('bt4_3','hs4_3','&#9655;','&#9661;');" title="reveal help"><span id="bt4_3" class="helpTitle onOff">▷</span></a> Computational Analysis</div>
	<div id="hs4_3" class="conceal helpContent">Total RNA was analysed using the Tuxedo pipeline (Trapnell <em>et al.</em> (2012) <em>Nature Protocols</em> <strong>7</strong>, 562–578). ’Liftover’ of genes with loci in the Tcas5.2 assembly to the icTricast1.1 assembly for display in the UCSC genome browser was done using the Liftoff tool (https://github.com/agshumate/Liftoff).</div>
